# Restricted Goods: Shipping & Compliance Notes

## Lithium-ion batteries

Products containing lithium-ion batteries (or shipped with them, e.g. as a bundled power bank)
are classified as Class 9 dangerous goods for air transport under IATA/ICAO regulations.

- **UN38.3 testing**: batteries must pass UN38.3 certification testing (covering altitude
  simulation, thermal, vibration, shock, and short-circuit tests) before they can legally be
  shipped by air. The supplier or a third-party lab must provide a UN38.3 test summary report.
- **State of charge**: for air transport, lithium-ion cells/batteries are typically required to
  be shipped at 30% or less state of charge to reduce fire risk.
- **Labeling**: shipments require Class 9 hazard labeling and a Lithium Battery handling label:
  omitting this is one of the most common causes of shipments being held or refused at origin.
- **Sea freight alternative**: ocean freight has fewer restrictions than air freight and is
  often used specifically to avoid air dangerous-goods surcharges and paperwork, at the cost of
  longer transit time.

## Medical device components

Products containing components intended for medical or health-monitoring use (even indirectly,
e.g. a sensor module later integrated into a certified medical device by the buyer) may trigger
destination-country regulatory review:

- In the US, this can mean FDA review depending on the device classification and intended use.
- In the EU, CE marking under the Medical Device Regulation (MDR) may apply.
- Even when the *finished* product is not itself a regulated medical device, importing
  medical-grade components can still require additional customs documentation confirming
  intended end use.

## Cosmetics

Cosmetic products (and raw materials intended for cosmetic formulation) typically require:

- Full ingredient (INCI) disclosure documentation.
- Destination-country product registration before the product can be legally sold — in the EU,
  this means registration in the Cosmetic Products Notification Portal (CPNP); other markets
  have their own equivalent registries.
- Safety assessment documentation, particularly for any product intended for use near the eyes,
  on children, or as a leave-on (rather than rinse-off) product.

*This document summarizes common compliance considerations at a high level and is not a
substitute for consulting the specific current regulations of the destination country or a
licensed customs broker.*

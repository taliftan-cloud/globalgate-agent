# Customs Classification (HS Code) Basics

## What an HS code is

The Harmonized System (HS) is an internationally standardized system of names and numbers for
classifying traded products, maintained by the World Customs Organization. The first 6 digits
are standardized globally; countries append additional digits (typically to 8 or 10 total) for
their own tariff schedules.

## Why classification matters

- **Duty rate**: the HS code directly determines the customs duty rate applied to a shipment.
  Two visually similar products can carry very different duty rates depending on material
  composition, function, or intended use — for example, a plastic toy versus a plastic
  household item can fall under different chapters with different rates.
- **Regulatory triggers**: certain HS codes automatically flag a shipment for additional
  scrutiny or required certifications (e.g. codes associated with electronics, toys intended
  for children under 12, or products containing batteries).
- **Trade agreement eligibility**: preferential duty rates under free trade agreements are
  determined by HS code plus a country-of-origin determination — an incorrect code can cause a
  shipment to miss out on a lower negotiated rate it would otherwise qualify for.

## Common misclassification risks

1. **Multi-function products** — a product combining functions (e.g. a Bluetooth speaker with a
   built-in power bank) may be classifiable under more than one chapter; the "essential
   character" of the product determines the correct single classification.
2. **Material composition changes** — a supplier substituting materials (e.g. switching from
   cotton to a cotton-polyester blend) between production runs can silently change the correct
   HS code without either party realizing it.
3. **Kits and sets** — products shipped as a kit with accessories may be classified differently
   than the same components shipped separately.

## Importer of record responsibility

The importer of record is legally responsible for the accuracy of the declared HS code and
customs value, even when a broker or freight forwarder prepares the paperwork — reliance on a
third party does not eliminate liability for penalties resulting from misclassification.

*This document is a general educational overview. Formal binding classification rulings should
be obtained from the destination country's customs authority (e.g. a US CBP binding ruling) for
high-value or high-volume products where classification risk matters.*
